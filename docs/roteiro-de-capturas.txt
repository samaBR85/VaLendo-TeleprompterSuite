## Opening

ALEX
Good evening, and welcome to the programme. Tonight we look at how a small change in the control room ends up changing what the audience sees at home.

[camera 2 · slow push in]

We have two stories, one guest in the studio, and a question we would like you to think about while you watch.

JORDAN
And it is a simple question. If the tools you use every day were built by the people who use them, what would be different?

## The first story

ALEX
It begins in a room the size of a wardrobe, with two monitors and a machine that nobody wanted to touch during a live show.

[roll VT · 42 seconds]

The producer told us she had learned to type without breathing. One wrong key and the text on the glass would jump, and the presenter would lose the line.

JORDAN
That is the part people outside the studio never see. The script is not a document. It is a thing that moves while somebody reads it out loud.

## The guest

ALEX
Our guest tonight has spent twenty years on the other side of the glass, reading what someone else typed.

[guest enters · handshake · seat]

JORDAN
Welcome. Let us start with the obvious one. What do you notice, from where you sit, when something goes wrong behind the camera?

## The second story

ALEX
The second story takes us out of the studio entirely, to a school hall with a laptop, a borrowed screen, and eleven students.

They had no budget and no equipment. What they had was a wireless network and a room full of devices already in their pockets.

JORDAN
By the end of the afternoon every one of those screens was showing the same page, at the same word. Nothing was installed. Nothing was paired.

## Closing

ALEX
That is all we have time for tonight.

[music under · fade to titles]

JORDAN
Thank you for watching. We will see you at the same time next week.
